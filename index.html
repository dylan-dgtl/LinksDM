Website Copy

Hero Section Headline

Generate a handful of headline and subheadline options for a website's hero section.

Detailed

Write 5 headline and subheadline pairs for the homepage hero section of a [type of business] website. The business is called [business name] and its main service is [main service]. The target customer is [describe customer]. Each headline should be under 10 words and clearly communicate the main benefit. Each subheadline should be one sentence that builds trust or reduces hesitation. Avoid generic phrases like "welcome to" or "unlock your potential."

SEO

Blog Post Outline

Turn a topic into a structured, SEO-friendly blog outline before you start writing.

Detailed

Create a blog post outline for the topic: [topic]. The target keyword is [keyword] and the audience is [describe audience]. Include a suggested title under 60 characters, a meta description under 155 characters, an intro angle, 4-6 H2 sections with 2-3 supporting bullet points each, and 3 frequently asked questions to include near the end. Keep the structure focused on what the reader actually wants to know, not just keyword coverage.

Instagram Caption Writer

Draft on-brand captions for a photo or promo post, with hook and hashtag options.

Write 3 Instagram caption options for a post about [what the post is showing]. The business is [business name], a [type of business]. Tone should be [describe tone, e.g. friendly and confident]. Each caption should open with a hook in the first line, be under 150 words, and end with a clear call to action. Suggest 8 relevant hashtags separately at the end.

Business

Client Proposal Email

Draft a follow-up email after a discovery call, summarizing scope and next steps.

Detailed

Write a follow-up email to a potential client after a discovery call. The client is interested in [service]. Key points discussed were: [list 2-4 points from the call]. The email should thank them for their time, briefly recap what was discussed, outline the proposed next step, and include a clear call to action with a suggested date. Keep the tone professional and warm, under 200 words, no exclamation marks.

Planning

Turn a Vague Goal into an Execution Plan

Turn a vague goal into concrete milestones, weekly actions, and metrics you can track.

Act as an experienced strategist and project manager. Turn this goal into a clear execution plan: [INSERT GOAL]. Define what success looks like, the single most important outcome to focus on, and the 3 to 5 biggest milestones needed to get there. For each milestone, list the specific actions required, the order to tackle them in, and a realistic timeframe. Then build a simple week-by-week outline for the next 12 weeks showing what to focus on and the top 3 priorities each week, plus 3 to 5 measurable metrics I can track to know if I'm on pace. Keep the plan practical and realistic, and don't load it with unnecessary tasks.

Act as an experienced strategist, project manager, and accountability coach. I have a goal, but it is currently too broad: My goal: [INSERT GOAL] Examples: Grow my business Find a new job Build a personal brand Launch a website Get more clients Turn this vague goal into a clear, realistic execution plan. Start by defining: What success should look like The most important outcome I should focus on The assumptions or missing information that could affect the plan The 3 to 5 biggest milestones required to reach the goal Then break each milestone into: Specific actions Priority level Recommended order Dependencies A realistic timeframe Create a 12-week plan that shows: What I should focus on each week The 3 highest-priority actions for that week What I should measure What progress should look like by the end of the week Give me a simple weekly scorecard with 3 to 5 measurable metrics I can track. Separate the metrics into: Leading indicators: actions I can directly control Lagging indicators: results those actions should eventually produce Also identify: The biggest distractions I should avoid The most likely reasons this plan could fail What I should do if I fall behind Anything I should stop doing to make room for the goal Finish with: My goal rewritten as a specific outcome My first 3 actions My 12-week roadmap My weekly scorecard A 30-minute weekly review process Keep the plan practical and achievable. Do not overwhelm me with unnecessary tasks. If my goal is still too vague to build a useful plan, ask me the minimum number of questions needed before proceeding.

Social Media

Better Social Posts

Find a sharp angle first, then get 3 ready-to-post drafts plus 5 alternative hooks.

Write a social media post about [INSERT TOPIC] for [LinkedIn / X / Threads / Instagram]. The audience is [INSERT AUDIENCE] and the goal is to [educate / get replies / drive clicks / build authority / get saves]. Open with a strong hook in the first line, focus on one clear idea, and end with a simple question or call to action. Keep the tone conversational and specific, skip clickbait and corporate jargon, and give me 2 versions: one educational, one more conversational.

Act as an experienced social media strategist and copywriter. Turn the following idea into a social media post that feels useful, original, human, and worth engaging with: Topic: [INSERT TOPIC] Platform: [LinkedIn / X / Threads / Instagram] Audience: [INSERT AUDIENCE] Goal: [Educate / Get replies / Drive clicks / Build authority / Get saves] Before writing the post, identify 3 possible angles that would make the topic more interesting. Prioritize angles that are: * Specific * Useful * Slightly unexpected * Easy to understand * Relevant to the audience Then choose the strongest angle and write the post. Structure it with: * A strong opening hook * One clear central idea * Useful examples or takeaways * Short, natural paragraphs * A conversational tone * A simple CTA or question at the end Avoid: * Generic motivational language * Clickbait * Corporate jargon * Overusing emojis * Overly dramatic one-line sentences * Sounding like an ad Give me 3 versions: 1. Educational 2. Conversational 3. Slightly more opinionated Also provide 5 alternative hooks I can test.

Content Strategy

Find the Idea Everyone Believes But Nobody Says

Uncover the unspoken opinions in your industry and turn them into scroll-stopping LinkedIn post angles.

Act as a cultural researcher and content strategist. My industry is [YOUR INDUSTRY] and my audience is [YOUR AUDIENCE]. Research current discussions, forums, Reddit, and social media in my niche, and find opinions people clearly believe but rarely say publicly, including common frustrations nobody addresses directly and industry practices people complain about but keep doing. Give me the 5 strongest opportunities, and for each one include the unspoken opinion, why people care about it, the conventional wisdom it challenges, and a strong LinkedIn post angle with a scroll-stopping opening line.

You are a cultural researcher and content strategist. I work in: [YOUR INDUSTRY] My audience is: [YOUR AUDIENCE] Search current discussions, articles, forums, Reddit conversations, social media discussions and industry communities related to my niche. I want you to identify ideas people clearly believe or experience but rarely say publicly. Look specifically for: * Opinions people repeatedly agree with in comments * Common frustrations nobody seems to address directly * Advice that sounds good publicly but people privately disagree with * Industry practices people complain about but continue doing * Emerging beliefs that challenge conventional wisdom * Questions being asked repeatedly without satisfying answers * Contrarian opinions supported by real evidence or experience Give me the 10 strongest opportunities. For each one, include: * The unspoken opinion * Why people care about it * Evidence that the conversation already exists * The conventional opinion it challenges * A strong LinkedIn post angle * A scroll-stopping opening line * A question that could create genuine discussion Rank the ideas from strongest to weakest based on: * Relevance * Emotional resonance * Originality * Discussion potential * Likelihood someone would share or save it Avoid manufactured controversy and engagement bait. I want ideas that make someone stop scrolling and think: "I've thought this for years, but I've never heard anyone actually say it."
